The short version

Straight answers

Six questions from your note, answered from your own numbers.

Is my pricing the problem?

No

Per serving, the Cacao bag ($3.67) and sticks ($4.50) sit at or below Ka'Chava ($4.66), the closest premium shake. Your Meta landing-page offer, $76.99 delivered for 30 servings, is one of the best first-order deals in the market. You only look expensive next to bone-broth and collagen tubs ($1.67 to $3.00 a serving), and that is who Google puts you beside.

Is it my costs?

Not product costs

A bag costs $29.04 to make and leaves $69 to $75 after shipping, box and card fees at full price. The problem is spending. Since July, marketing has averaged $8,690 a month, more than the $7,110 of gross profit it helped bring in, before any other overhead. In October the marketing line was $7,074 and the store gained 17 new customers.

Do people just want free shipping?

Unproven, so test it

Cheaper shipping didn't raise checkout completion, and shoppers who type an email finish at the same rate with or without a shipping charge. But most people who leave checkout never type an email, so that data can't see them. Test it at $75: every bag and stick pack qualifies, even with a 15% code. With no extra orders it costs $77 to $208 a month. It pays for itself at 2 to 4 extra orders a month, and I'd expect 3 to 5.

Should the 7-Day cost more per serving?

Yes

Smaller packs should cost more per serving, and yours is set right: 56% above the bag per serving, against a market median of 57% for single-serve formats. Keep $39.99. What hurts is the $10.23 of shipping on top, which takes it to $7.17 a serving.

Is the $150 free-shipping line doing anything?

No

In 90 days, 36 web orders landed between $99 and $149.99 and only 8 between $150 and $175, so it isn't pulling carts up. Every core product sits under it, and a bag plus a 7-Day comes to $149.98, two cents short.

So what would make money?

Volume, not price

Run the holiday free-shipping test. If shipping turns out to matter, fix renewal shipping next. Put the real effort into what a customer costs to win and how long they stay. A 10% price rise on everything would add about $800 a month at October volume, and October lost $12,882.

Shipping

Shipping is the weakest part of the offer

Your shipping charges run above your own label costs and above what rivals charge, renewals pay it too, and the $150 line doesn't change what people buy.

You charge more than shipping costs you

Your rates are Shopify's USPS price plus a $2.00 handling fee. Shoppers who paid shipping on a single item paid $11.95 (sticks) to $12.39 (bag) on average. The real label averaged $8.88 for sticks and $9.52 for a bag. Rivals that charge under their line charge a flat $7.95 to $9.99.

Renewals pay shipping too

78 of 102 renewals in the last 90 days paid shipping, $11.37 on average. The "free shipping on subscriptions over $119.99" discount set up in March never reaches renewals: all 35 renewals between $119.99 and $149.99 paid shipping, because renewals are re-priced at the store's normal rates. Only renewals of $150 or more ship free. Funnel subscriptions do ship free.

The $150 line doesn't lift carts

36 web orders landed at $99 to $149.99 and 8 at $150 to $174.99 in 90 days. Every core product is under the line. A bag plus a 7-Day comes to $149.98. A bag plus a shaker is $125.98.

What the checkout data can and can't see

Shoppers who typed an email finished 74% of the time with or without a shipping charge, and the move to cheaper Ground shipping didn't change that (75.9% before, 72.1% after). But in October 102 store sessions reached checkout and 31 finished. Most of the 71 who left never typed an email, so we can't see what stopped them. Across US shoppers, extra costs such as shipping are the most common reason for leaving a checkout (Baymard Institute).

Holiday test

The holiday free-shipping test

Six weeks, right after Black Friday, new orders only, at $75. A small, capped bet that answers whether free shipping wins customers.

What
Free US shipping on orders of $75 or more: one-time orders and the first order of a new subscription. Renewals stay as they are. Every bag, stick pack and 4-pack qualifies, even after a 15% to 30% code. A 7-Day on its own doesn't; two do.
When
Tuesday Dec 1, 2026, 12:00 am CT to Monday Jan 11, 2027, 11:59 pm CT. It starts the day after Black Friday pricing ends and covers holiday gifting and New Year demand. Your 13 Black Friday discounts are set not to combine with shipping discounts, so running both in the same week would make some shoppers pick one or the other, and the 30% sale would blur the read.
How it's set up
One automatic free-shipping discount: US only, $75 minimum, one-time orders on, subscriptions on for the first order only, combines with product and order discounts, with a start and end date. The shipping rates themselves don't change, so it switches itself off and can't leak into renewals.
Tell people
Top bar and cart bar ($150 to $75 for the test), the shipping line on product pages, emails, ads, and Merchant Center shipping so Google Shopping shows free shipping.
Cost
With no extra orders: $77 to $208 a month in shipping you'd otherwise collect ($77 at the last 30 days' volume, $208 at the last 90 days'), so about $105 to $290 over the six weeks.
Break-even
About 2 to 4 extra orders a month, which is 5% to 8% more web orders.
Baseline
Oct 13 to Nov 23, the six weeks before Black Friday. Internal test sessions on Nov 4–5 and Nov 10–11 are excluded.
Watch weekly
Sessions, add-to-cart rate, checkout starts, orders per 1,000 sessions, new customers, average order, shipping given up, margin per order.
Keep it if
New customers per 1,000 sessions run at least 15% above the baseline and margin per 1,000 sessions holds. Then make $75+ permanent and decide on renewal shipping. If not, go back to today's setup.
Read it carefully
Holiday traffic isn't October traffic and volume is small. Treat a gap under 15% as noise.
